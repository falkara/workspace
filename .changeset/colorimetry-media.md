`Colorimetry` holds the media a color is written in: `srgb` and `displayP3` after CSS Color 4, `rec2020` after ITU-R BT.2020-2 with the display curve of ITU-R BT.1886-0, and `rec2100Pq` and `rec2100Hlg` after ITU-R BT.2100-3 and CSS Color HDR. Each is a `Medium`, plain data compared by value: its primaries and its white as the standard writes them, and the matrices between its linear channels and XYZ derived from them exactly in `Rational`, which for sRGB, Display P3 and Rec. 2020 equal those of CSS Color 4 entry for entry. `onExactWhite` builds any medium on the exact white of the library instead, for physical exactness, at the cost of differing from the browsers in the fifth decimal place. The types keep apart what differs physically, at no cost at run time: a `Medium` carries its color space and its white, `'standard'` or `'exact'`, both of which default to any; a `Matrix` carries the spaces it converts between and a `Vector` its space, so that `transform` applies a matrix only to a vector of the space it converts from, and channels worked out in one medium, or on one white, are refused by the matrices of another; `xyzOf` gives the vector in XYZ of a color. A vector or a matrix written by hand fits any space. `isInGamut` decides exactly whether a color lies within a medium, and `write` writes it in the CSS syntax of the medium through its transfer function. BT.2100 maps the library's white to HDR Reference White, 203 cd/m² after ITU-R BT.2408-9, and with PQ reaches up to 10 000 cd/m², each a `Luminance`, branded apart from the peak of a medium and other values relative to its white, a `RelativeToWhite`; how far HLG reaches above the white is left to the rule for luminance.
