`Colorimetry` gives a hue by a wavelength, as CIE S 017 defines it: `dominantWavelength` for λd and `complementaryWavelength` for λc, each from the wavelength and the color-matching functions x̄, ȳ and z̄ of the CIE 1931 2° observer at it, which the caller supplies as the CIE dataset publishes them. Both return a `Hue`, held in XYZ, where lights add as vectors: its `spectralLight` is the spectral light S = (x̄, ȳ, z̄) as published, a `Tristimulus`, and its `direction` is S for a dominant wavelength or its opposite, −S, for a complementary one, a `Direction`. A direction is a change of a color rather than a light, so its type is branded apart from a color: `add` adds it to a color and `scale` scales it, but it cannot be written, tested for a gamut or given a chromaticity. The colors of the hue are the mixtures α·W + β·D of the white and that direction, with α > 0 and β ≥ 0, so that a color of a complementary wavelength, mixed with its spectral light, gives white. Their inputs, `DominantWavelengthOptions` and `ComplementaryWavelengthOptions`, refuse a wavelength outside 360 nm to 830 nm, a negative value, a row of zeros and a spectral light parallel to the white, decided exactly, each with its reason. The chromaticity plane is only a view: `chromaticityOf` gives the chromaticity of a color, such as that of the spectral light, `chromaticityOf(hue.spectralLight)`, or none for black, and `whiteChromaticity` is now typed as the new `Chromaticity`.
