`Colorimetry` gives a hue by a wavelength, as CIE S 017 defines it: `dominantWavelength` for λd and `complementaryWavelength` for λc, each from the wavelength and the color-matching functions x̄, ȳ and z̄ of the CIE 1931 2° observer at it, which the caller supplies as the CIE dataset publishes them. Both return a `Hue`, held in XYZ, where lights add as vectors: its `direction` is the spectral light S = (x̄, ȳ, z̄) as published for a dominant wavelength, or its opposite, −S, for a complementary one. The colors of the hue are the mixtures α·W + β·D of the white and that direction, with α > 0 and β ≥ 0, so that a color of a complementary wavelength, mixed with its spectral light, gives white. Their inputs, `DominantWavelengthOptions` and `ComplementaryWavelengthOptions`, refuse a wavelength outside 360 nm to 830 nm, a negative value, a row of zeros and a spectral light parallel to the white, decided exactly, each with its reason. The chromaticity plane is only a view: `chromaticityOf` gives the chromaticity of a color, or none for black, the same for S and −S, and `whiteChromaticity` is now typed as the new `Chromaticity`.
