# @falkara/presentation

## 0.10.0

### Minor Changes

- [`6d3f3e9`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/6d3f3e9a3856dd9aabc5e2218d99126f12cdd581) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - `Colorimetry` defines a color apart from the media it is written in. A `Definition` gives, for any medium, a color proven to lie in its gamut, an `InGamut` of that medium, or a failure with the reason, and computes nothing until it is used. `purest({ hue, headroom })` defines the purest color of a hue in every medium, with `whiteLevel`, 1, as the default headroom; `define` takes a rule of the project's own, an effect with no requirements that ends in `inGamut`; and `byMedium` gives a definition exceptions for some media, keyed by the names of `media`, matched by color space on either white, with an unknown name refused by the compiler. `inMedium` evaluates a definition in one medium, and `inMedia` and `writeMedia` in every medium of `media`, or of a record a project passes, such as the media on the exact white, keeping its names; a failure names every medium it arose in, as a `FailedInMedium`. `inGamut` decides exactly whether a color lies in the gamut of a medium and names the channel that leaves it, as an `OutOfGamut`.

  What consumers of 0.9.0 change:

  - `purestColor({ hue, medium, headroom })` becomes `inMedium(purest({ hue, headroom }), medium)`, and `PurestColorOptions` becomes `PurestOptions`, without the medium. A brand written for every medium becomes `writeMedia(purest({ hue }))`.
  - `write` takes only a color proven to lie in the gamut of the same medium, on the same white, as `inMedium` or `inGamut` gives it, so that nothing reaches CSS outside a gamut.
  - `isInGamut(medium, color)` becomes `Exit.isSuccess(inGamut(medium, color))`, which also gives the reason a color lies outside.

## 0.9.0

### Minor Changes

- [`aad8bb3`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/aad8bb3ea3792aef5c078380bb8da06afee93d7c) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - A new module, `Colorimetry`, holds the white every color of the library will rest on: the CIE standard illuminant D65 under the CIE 1931 standard colorimetric observer for a 2° field. Only its tristimulus values relative to a Y of 1, `whiteTristimulus`, are worked out once, as a `Tristimulus`, the type of a color, branded apart from other values of the same shape and made from values a caller writes with `tristimulus`, exactly, from the spectrum of D65 and the color-matching functions of the observer in the official CIE datasets, which are cited with their checksums. Its chromaticity coordinates, `whiteChromaticity`, follow from them in the code. D65 is taken as its spectrum rather than as published chromaticity coordinates, which follow from the spectrum and an observer, so that a later observer keeps the same white and only its tristimulus values are worked out again.

- [`3d0cdf6`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/3d0cdf6bc4438d2c15f1314928f4acd280fb465d) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - `Colorimetry` holds the media a color is written in: `srgb` and `displayP3` after CSS Color 4, `rec2020` after ITU-R BT.2020-2 with the display curve of ITU-R BT.1886-0, and `rec2100Pq` and `rec2100Hlg` after ITU-R BT.2100-3 and CSS Color HDR. Each is a `Medium`, plain data compared by value: its primaries and its white as the standard writes them, and the matrices between its linear channels and XYZ derived from them exactly in `Rational`, which for sRGB, Display P3 and Rec. 2020 equal those of CSS Color 4 entry for entry. `onExactWhite` builds any medium on the exact white of the library instead, for physical exactness, at the cost of differing from the browsers in the fifth decimal place. The types keep apart what differs physically, at no cost at run time: a `Medium` carries its color space and its white, `'standard'` or `'exact'`, both of which default to any; a `Matrix` carries the spaces it converts between and a `Vector` its space, so that `transform` applies a matrix only to a vector of the space it converts from, and channels worked out in one medium, or on one white, are refused by the matrices of another; `xyzOf` gives the vector in XYZ of a color. A vector or a matrix written by hand fits any space. `isInGamut` decides exactly whether a color lies within a medium, and `write` writes it in the CSS syntax of the medium through its transfer function. BT.2100 maps the library's white to HDR Reference White, 203 cd/m² after ITU-R BT.2408-9, and with PQ reaches up to 10 000 cd/m², each a `Luminance`, branded apart from the peak of a medium and other values relative to its white, a `RelativeToWhite`; how far HLG reaches above the white is left to the rule for luminance.

- [`398efdb`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/398efdb99a50796ad7554423624db879c3fca4e6) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - The library no longer computes in floating point. Every value is exact: rational numbers in the `Rational` of Effect, and an irrational number, such as a root, a logarithm, a cosine or π, as bounds that narrow on demand until each decision is certain. What consumers gain:

  - `Colorimetry.write` encodes every medium exactly and writes each channel correctly rounded to `Colorimetry.decimalPlaces`, 5, the fewest that tell apart every step of a 16-bit channel, `Colorimetry.deepestChannelBits`, with a number exactly halfway rounded toward +∞, as CSS Color 4 rounds. The bound of 32 units in the last place is gone.
  - `Color` reads each number of `oklch()` exactly as the decimal it is written as, so `0.1` is 1/10, and converts it through the matrices of CSS Color 4 exactly. `contrast`, `verify` and the contrast in `ContrastShortfall` give the number JavaScript holds nearest to the exact contrast; black against white gives 20.999999999999993, as white falls a little short of a relative luminance of 1 by those matrices.
  - With `channelBits`, `reaches` and `verify` decide the contrast of rounded colors exactly, however close the ratio, by Besicovitch's theorem on sums of roots.
  - Without `channelBits`, the cosine and the sine of a hue make exact decisions on `oklch()` colors impossible to settle where they fall on their edge, so they follow one stated rule: a contrast or a channel closer to its edge than the smallest change a 16-bit channel shows counts as lying on it, and meets it. Black against white reaches 21:1 by that rule.
  - `shade` searches the lightnesses and chromas written with 5 decimal places, so that the shade written is exactly the shade judged, and passes over a lightness with a channel on the edge between two 8-bit steps, which tools computing in rounded numbers may round either way. Its output changes: `oklch(64% 0.17 147)` on white gives `oklch(0.4491 0.13347 147)`.
  - `Fluid` and `Typography` round every length exactly.
  - Exactness costs time, milliseconds per value, so the library is meant for build time, as the README now says; a test holds each value to a second.

  What changes for consumers:

  - The components of a decoded `Color` are `Rational`, and encoding writes them exactly in decimal, refusing a number whose decimal expansion never ends. A number whose exponent exceeds 10000 either way is refused, as `BigDecimal` reads no further.
  - The ratio of `reaches`, the `minimumContrast` of `Pair` and `Brand`, and the `requiredContrast` of `ContrastShortfall` and `NoLegibleShade` are `Rational`.
  - `enhancedContrastRatio`, `largeTextEnhancedContrastRatio`, `gamutTolerance`, `oklabToLms`, `lmsToXyz`, `fullChroma`, `luminanceWeights`, `contrastFlare`, `maximumContrastRatio`, `halfTurns` and `encodedTransferThreshold` are `Rational`; the matrices are `Colorimetry.Matrix`, `oklabToLms` from Oklab to the cube roots of LMS and `lmsToXyz` from LMS to XYZ, so that a vector of cube roots is refused by `lmsToXyz` until it is cubed.
  - `unitRoundoff` is removed, as nothing rounds. `xyzToLinearSrgb` is removed in favor of `Colorimetry.srgb.xyzToLinear`, which equals it entry for entry, and `transferThreshold`, `transferSlope`, `transferGamma` and `transferOffset` in favor of `Colorimetry.srgbTransfer`.
  - `Fluid.rem` and `Fluid.vw` take a `Rational`, as `Fluid.clamp` does.

- [`ef892f3`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/ef892f3dd8176d63656a8538c730eac343697935) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - `Colorimetry.purestColor` turns a hue into a color in a medium: the purest color of the hue the medium shows, at the highest luminance a headroom allows, as exact tristimulus values. In the medium, the linear channels of a color α·W + β·D of the hue are α·w + β·d, and the purest is the one with the largest β/α at which no channel is below 0, found in closed form, exactly in `Rational`. Every measure of purity CIE defines grows with β/α along a hue, so the result does not depend on which one is meant. Where the direction lowers no channel, the gamut holds the spectral light itself, and the purest color is that light alone: in Rec. 2020 and BT.2100, whose primaries are rounded coordinates, the gamut contains the spectral lights of 532 nm and 630 nm. The headroom bounds the largest linear channel relative to the white rather than the luminance Y; it is 1 by default, so that in HDR a color stays at the level of HDR Reference White, and may reach the peak of the medium, up to 10000/203 for PQ. It is a `RelativeToWhite`, written with `relativeToWhite` or taken from the peak of the medium, so that a luminance in cd/m² is refused by the compiler. `PurestColorOptions` refuses a headroom that is not positive or that exceeds the peak, each with its reason.

- [`0b39275`](https://github.com/falkara/workspace/commit/0b392751a5fbe0080a9fdc0aaeb22d064744a13b) Thanks [@filipfalcon](https://github.com/filipfalcon)! - `Colorimetry` gives a hue by a wavelength, as CIE S 017 defines it: `dominantWavelength` for λd and `complementaryWavelength` for λc, each from the wavelength and the color-matching functions x̄, ȳ and z̄ of the CIE 1931 2° observer at it, which the caller supplies as the CIE dataset publishes them. Both return a `Hue`, held in XYZ, where lights add as vectors: its `spectralLight` is the spectral light S = (x̄, ȳ, z̄) as published, a `Tristimulus`, and its `direction` is S for a dominant wavelength or its opposite, −S, for a complementary one, a `Direction`. A direction is a change of a color rather than a light, so its type is branded apart from a color: `add` adds it to a color and `scale` scales it, but it cannot be written, tested for a gamut or given a chromaticity. The colors of the hue are the mixtures α·W + β·D of the white and that direction, with α > 0 and β ≥ 0, so that a color of a complementary wavelength, mixed with its spectral light, gives white. Their inputs, `DominantWavelengthOptions` and `ComplementaryWavelengthOptions`, refuse a wavelength outside 360 nm to 830 nm, a negative value, a row of zeros and a spectral light parallel to the white, decided exactly, each with its reason. The chromaticity plane is only a view: `chromaticityOf` gives the chromaticity of a color, such as that of the spectral light, `chromaticityOf(hue.spectralLight)`, or none for black, and `whiteChromaticity` is now typed as the new `Chromaticity`.
